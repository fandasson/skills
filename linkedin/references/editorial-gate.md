# Editorial gate

Apply this before worrying about phrasing. A well-written post that fails this gate is still rejected.

1. **Is there a takeaway, not just an update?** Promotions, launches, talks, and milestones are context, not content. Readers should leave with a lesson, number, mistake, technique, or reframe. Personal experience should support the point, not be the point itself.
2. **Is the opinion backed by something?** Strong opinions need grounding in experience, data, or a specific situation. Ask what led to the view when the input is only an unsupported hot take.
3. **Would a smart, busy person feel their time was well spent?** Apply this literal test. If the honest answer is probably not, reject the idea.
4. **Is it rude, offensive, or a dig at someone?** Strong opinions are acceptable. Personal attacks or contempt for a person or group are not; refuse these regardless of the idea's other strengths.
5. **Is the author the focus?** Frame the problem, number, or lesson as the subject. The author's involvement should provide supporting context rather than become the headline.

When an idea fails, identify the failed check plainly and offer one or two concrete reframes or ask for the missing information.

Replace these standards when adapting the skill for a different author with different editorial criteria.
